# Relocation Checklist Formatting Skill

## When to use this

Use this skill whenever the employee's question is about an internal
relocation/transfer -- moving between Kestrel offices (Pune, Mumbai,
Bengaluru), what happens to pay, leave, equipment, or access when doing so,
or what to do before/during/after a confirmed move. Do NOT use it for
questions about business travel, expense claims unrelated to relocation, or
generic leave questions that don't involve moving offices.

## How to format the answer

Instead of a flat paragraph, lay the answer out as a **dated checklist**
organized into these phases (skip a phase entirely if nothing in the
findings applies to it -- don't invent placeholder steps):

1. **As soon as it's confirmed** -- eligibility and paperwork (e.g. Form
   HR-12, the 30-day-ahead submission window).
2. **In the weeks before the move** -- anything with a lead time attached
   (raising an IT "Desk Setup" ticket 10 working days ahead, booking travel
   for dependents).
3. **On moving day** -- what to physically do (carry the laptop in cabin
   baggage, hand over the old office's access card).
4. **In the first days/weeks at the new location** -- temporary
   accommodation window, new access card, new peripherals.
5. **Money that follows** -- when the relocation allowance and goods-shifting
   reimbursement actually land, and with what conditions.
6. **Know this before you sign anything** -- the 12-month recovery clause on
   early resignation, stated as a plain warning, not buried at the bottom.

Each checklist line must still carry its citation, e.g.:

- [ ] Submit Form HR-12 on the HR portal at least 30 days before the move
      date. `[KSPL-HR-POL-004, section 4.2]`

If a conflict was found between documents (e.g. temporary accommodation
nights), put the resolved value directly in the checklist line and add the
conflict explanation as a note directly beneath that line, not in a separate
section -- the employee reading a checklist wants the answer where they'll
act on it, not cross-referenced elsewhere.

End with a one-line summary of any gap (something no document answered),
exactly as the normal `gaps` rules require.
